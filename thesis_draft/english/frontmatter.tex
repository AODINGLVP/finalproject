\frontchapter{Abstract}
Visual behaviour trees are widely used to author game-agent decisions, but conventional node--link editors become difficult to inspect as trees grow and as runtime state changes. This dissertation presents the design and evaluation of a Godot 4.6 editor plugin that combines a complete executable behaviour-tree system with independently switchable techniques for large-tree readability and live debugging. The runtime supports ordered, reactive, random, parallel, repeating and timed control flow; actor method invocation; typed blackboard data; decorators; and editor-only execution inspection. The editor implements compact cards, semantic zoom, fisheye focus+context, subtree collapse and focus, search highlighting, overview+detail, stable layout, alternative edge routing, active-path emphasis and failure annotation.

Evaluation used same-generator 31-, 61-, 121-, 241- and 364-node trees, a playable 241-node enemy tree, automated semantic and GUI tests, and real-\ac{GPU} visual regression. Six display conditions received three warm-ups and 30 repetitions at each scale, producing 900 observations. Compact cards reduced summed card area by 61.09--61.35\% without hiding cards; semantic overview reduced it by 90.27--90.34\%; search dimmed 96.15--99.67\% of cards; and every condition retained zero overlap, saved positions and execution order. A versioned A1--B1--B2--A2 comparison added 200 fixed 240-step drags. Median processing time decreased at four of five scales, with bootstrap intervals wholly above zero at 241 and 364 nodes; the 364-node median fell from 2,939.47 to 1,758.30 ms (40.18\%). The strict pre-set criterion nevertheless failed because the 31-node point estimate regressed and its interval crossed zero. The playable resource reproduced the geometric effects. These are objective presentation and interaction results, not proof of human comprehension; human usability remains unverified.
